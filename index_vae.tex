\section{Introduction}\label{introduction}

The USFS Forest Inventory and Analysis (FIA) Program surveys and
analyzes the forests of the United States through measurements of forest
vegetation traits made at a nationwide network of permanent sample
locations. These plot locations are distributed across all lands with a
spatial sampling intensity of about 1 location per 2400 ha. Statistical
inference on forest population status and change parameters is enabled
by measurements made at these locations on schedules intended to be
fixed and consistent within states.

In FIA, each plot location in a state is assigned to one of a set of
panels, and all plots assigned to a single panel are designated for
measurement in the same year. Once the final panel in an inventory cycle
is complete, remeasurements at sample locations in the first panel begin
the following year. Delayed and skipped field measurements cause
unintentional lengthening of inventory cycles and/or survey nonresponse,
rates that vary regionally and over time. The underlying causes of
delayed and skipped field measurements are varied, and can be chronic
(e.g., denied access to private lands Westfall et al. (2022), logistical
or administrative challenges in remote regions Affleck et al. (2026),
persistent workforce and budgetary constraints) or result from discrete
events (e.g., the COVID19 pandemic). Better understanding of patterns in
late and skipped measurements, and the investigation of new sampling and
estimation techniques to ameliorate associated statistical consequences
over longer time periods, are active research topics.

Of immediate practical concern for FIA and its external partners are the
strain on field resources and delays to tabular data publication that
result from delayed field measurements. Methods have been proposed to
control the measurement delay process by disaggregating FIA's annual
panel structure in such a way that the spatial dispersion of the FIA
sample network is preserved within field season years. Panel
disaggregation enables intentional alignment of workloads with
anticipated data acquisition workforce capacity in each field season
year through the application of variable annual field sampling effort
within individual states.

Though the logistical advantages of variable annual effort (VAE) are
profound (i.e., it can help ensure the long-term viability of the
inventory in the context of funding and staffing volatility), the
statistical consequences of the redistribution of partial panels within
inventory cycles, and of extended and variable inventory cycle lengths,
are unknown across states and epochs. In particular, a need exists to
understand and communicate to stakeholders how adoption of VAE protocols
may influence the statistical properties of forest parameter estimates
published by FIA in fulfillment of Congressional reporting imperatives.
